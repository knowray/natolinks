Country Dossiers · Launch set

Who is building the capacity?

These dossiers do not rank loyalty. They identify the geographic role, force-development question, industrial base and public policy choices that matter to the Alliance transition.

How to read a dossier

From national policy to alliance effect

Each country page is designed to follow the same chain: stated requirement → budget authority → programme → supplier/production → fielded force → remaining dependency. The short launch entries below are a substantive starting point; they will expand into sourced, dated records as the Monitor is populated.

Not a league table

A country may be strong in one mission and dependent in another. The useful question is how each contribution combines with allies: northern surveillance, eastern land defence, air defence, logistics, maritime protection, munitions or command.

Eastern flank · Land forces

Poland

Poland sits at the Alliance’s most demanding conventional-defence frontier. Its central issue is not whether it recognizes the Russian threat; it is whether large procurement programmes can be integrated into trained, sustained and networked formations.

  • What to watch: force integration, air and missile defence, ammunition and maintenance depth.
  • Alliance role: forward land defence, host-nation support and a logistics bridge to the east.
  • Evidence trail: national procurement decisions, NATO force deployments, exercises and delivery schedules.

Central Europe · Industrial scale

Germany

Germany is pivotal because its geography, economy and industrial base make it essential to moving, sustaining and manufacturing for Europe. The live question is delivery speed: turning policy commitments into deployable formations, ammunition and resilient logistics.

  • What to watch: readiness of heavy formations, air defence, ammunition orders and infrastructure for military mobility.
  • Alliance role: central logistics hub, framework nation and industrial anchor.
  • Evidence trail: Bundeswehr procurement, budget implementation, industry output and NATO commitments.

Strategic autonomy · Expeditionary power

France

France brings a full-spectrum national military, a nuclear deterrent independent of NATO command, major aerospace and land-systems industry, and experience in expeditionary operations. NATOlinks tracks how those strengths translate into collective European conventional capacity.

  • What to watch: air/missile defence, munitions, naval availability and European programme cooperation.
  • Alliance role: high-end command, air, maritime and land contribution.
  • Evidence trail: Military Programming Law, procurement milestones and readiness reporting.

Northern Atlantic · Maritime and air

United Kingdom

The UK remains one of Europe’s most capable military actors: nuclear deterrence, maritime reach, combat aviation, intelligence relationships and a deep defence-industrial base. The practical transition test is capacity: fleet, munitions, personnel and sustained readiness.

  • What to watch: North Atlantic and High North posture, air power, shipbuilding, ammunition and deployable land force.
  • Alliance role: maritime security, nuclear deterrence, rapid reinforcement and command.
  • Evidence trail: Strategic Defence Review, Ministry of Defence plans and programme delivery.

Nordic–Baltic · Arctic readiness

Sweden & Finland

Finland and Sweden changed the map of northern European defence. Their membership connects experienced territorial-defence, Arctic and Baltic expertise to NATO planning; it also makes movement, air defence, maritime awareness and reinforcement in the High North more integrated questions.

  • What to watch: Nordic command integration, airfields, maritime surveillance, reserves and cross-border mobility.
  • Alliance role: northern flank defence, resilient society and cold-weather capability.
  • Evidence trail: national defence plans, Nordic exercises and NATO regional planning.

North Sea · Mobility and enablement

Netherlands & Denmark

Smaller Allies can matter disproportionately when they bring high-readiness formations, maritime access, precision capabilities or the ability to move allied forces quickly. The Netherlands and Denmark illustrate why readiness is not simply a function of national size.

  • What to watch: maritime protection, F-35 and air-defence integration, port/rail/road capacity and multinational formations.
  • Alliance role: mobility, logistics, North Sea access and high-end interoperable contributions.
  • Evidence trail: force-generation announcements, exercises and host-nation agreements.

Baltic · Forward defence

Estonia, Latvia & Lithuania

The Baltic Allies sit where warning time is short and reinforcement credibility is essential. Their contribution combines territorial defence, host-nation support, resilience and the ability to make allied forces effective on arrival.

Watch: air defence, reserves, protected infrastructure, rail and port access, ammunition stocks and multinational integration.

Northern flank · Sea and air approaches

Norway & Iceland

Norway provides the Alliance a critical view of the High North, northern maritime approaches and the Russian bastion. Iceland’s geography and facilities make it important to North Atlantic air and maritime operations.

Watch: maritime awareness, anti-submarine warfare, airfield resilience, cold-weather sustainment and allied access.

Central Europe · Enablers

Belgium, Luxembourg & Czechia

These Allies illustrate the value of connected enablers: air power, multinational force contributions, logistics, specialised land forces and an industrial or geographic role within Europe’s reinforcement network.

Watch: readiness of deployable forces, ammunition, mobility corridors, F-35 integration and industrial supply chains.

Eastern flank · Rear area to front

Romania, Bulgaria & Slovakia

The southeastern and central-eastern Allies link land, air and maritime theatres. Their readiness matters both for regional defence and for the routes through which other European forces reinforce the flank.

Watch: air-defence coverage, Black Sea and Danube access, brigade infrastructure, mobility, training areas and host-nation support.

Black Sea gateway · Strategic access

Türkiye

Türkiye brings large armed forces, a major defence-industrial base, control of the Turkish Straits and a unique position between Black Sea, Mediterranean and Middle Eastern theatres. Alliance value and political friction must both be assessed in public view.

Watch: Black Sea posture, air and naval capacity, drone and missile production, basing access and interoperability choices.

Mediterranean · Maritime and air hub

Italy, Spain & Portugal

Southern European Allies provide Mediterranean basing, naval and air power, industry, Atlantic access and reinforcement routes. Their role connects European conventional defence to maritime security, Africa-facing pressure and transatlantic logistics.

Watch: naval availability, air bases, shipbuilding, air defence, Atlantic ports and expeditionary readiness.

Adriatic & Balkan · Regional stability

Greece, Croatia, Slovenia, Albania, Montenegro & North Macedonia

These Allies sit on important southern transport routes and contribute to the political and military stability of the Western Balkans and eastern Mediterranean. National scale varies; geographic access and coalition interoperability remain central.

Watch: port and airfield access, air policing, regional exercises, mobility and defence-modernisation delivery.

North American anchor

United States & Canada

The United States remains a decisive ally for deterrence, high-end enabling capabilities, nuclear assurance and transatlantic reinforcement. Canada combines North American, Arctic and European commitments. NATO 3.0 asks where European capacity can assume a greater conventional burden without treating either ally as peripheral.

Watch: forward posture, strategic lift, ISR, munitions, Arctic infrastructure and European burden-sharing outcomes.
