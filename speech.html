Case file · 18 June 2026 · Brussels

Hegseth’s NATO 3.0 speech

The speech that turned a familiar burden-sharing dispute into a concrete review of force posture, basing and alliance reliability.

Suggested editorial image: official NATO photo gallery · NATO credits required · editorial use only.

01 · THE DIAGNOSIS

“Paper tiger” and one-way street

Hegseth argued that NATO had drifted from hard-power collective defence and that Europe had allowed its conventional capacity to decline while relying on U.S. protection.

02 · THE HISTORICAL CLAIM

Back to a balanced Alliance

He invoked NATO 1.0 and Eisenhower to argue that Europe should lead its own conventional defence alongside a strong United States.

03 · THE ACCESS DISPUTE

A test some Allies allegedly failed

Hegseth said that some Allies did not grant requested, predictable access involving bases, ports or overflight for U.S. operations against Iran. He said assets had to shift elsewhere.

Important distinction The speech did not publicly name every country or disclose the agreements at issue. Basing and overflight remain sovereign permissions, not a generic American “right.”

04 · THE POLICY RESPONSE

The NATO 3.0 review

He announced an up-to-six-month review of U.S. force posture and basing in Europe, designed to support a Europe-led conventional-defence model and clearer access arrangements.

Why this matters

The issue is not only spending. The operating test of an alliance is whether forces can move, be hosted, supplied, sustained and politically supported in a crisis.
