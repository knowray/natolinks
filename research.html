Evidence before argument

NATOlinks is meant to be a gateway into serious work—not a substitute for it. These institutions form the initial research shelf for alliance policy, force design, industry and Europe’s changing security environment.

Source discipline

Primary sources first; analysis clearly labelled.

Every factual country, programme or capability record should begin with official treaty text, government budget/contract material, NATO releases or official force statements. Research institutes add context and critique. News reporting identifies developments and competing claims, but does not replace primary evidence.

Three labels used on NATOlinks

Documented fact — supported by a linked primary or high-quality source.

Government or company claim — attributed, not adopted as fact.

Analytical inference — a clearly explained judgement from public evidence.

Build standard

What “source-led” means on a NATOlinks page

Readers should be able to trace a claim, see its date, know whether it is official or analytical, and distinguish an intention from a delivered result. The site will not claim access to confidential basing arrangements, classified readiness data or private supplier information. Its value is organizing the public record clearly enough that the real gaps become visible.
