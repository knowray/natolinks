Programme tracker · Operating model

From announcement to usable force

A procurement announcement is not a military capability. NATOlinks records the stages that turn political intent into a system a unit can actually operate, sustain and replace.

01 · Requirement

What gap is being closed?

Name the operational problem in plain language: defend a port, detect a missile, move a brigade, replenish a launcher, protect a command node or keep an airfield open. The requirement must connect to a deterrence or reinforcement function—not simply a platform brand.

02 · Decision

Who has committed?

Record the political decision, responsible ministry, budget line, procurement authority, partners and stated schedule. A pledge, framework agreement and signed production contract are different stages and must never be merged.

03 · Production

Can the supply chain deliver?

Track prime contractor, key sub-suppliers, production location, order backlog, component exposure, licensing and reload or spares arrangements. Production resilience can matter more than the first delivery.

04 · Integration

Does it work in the alliance system?

A new system has to be crewed, trained, networked, sustained and compatible with allied command-and-control. Interoperability, ammunition commonality and maintenance belong to delivery, not the footnotes.

05 · Readiness

When is it genuinely usable?

Initial operating capability and full operating capability are not interchangeable. A record must note stockpiles, dispersal, protection, mobility, maintenance cycles and the ability to operate under electronic attack or interrupted supply.

06 · Residual risk

What still depends on someone else?

Identify external dependencies: foreign munitions, U.S. enablers, a single factory, imported components, limited trained personnel, fragile infrastructure or untested wartime command arrangements.

NATO 3.0 test

The question is not whether Europe buys more.

It is whether European states can assume greater conventional responsibility while remaining integrated with the United States and Canada. That requires programmes that can be fielded at scale, maintained across borders and replenished under pressure. The tracker prioritises air defence, deep fires, munitions, drones, ISR, mobility, maritime security and command networks.

Tracker labels

Declared — political intent.

Contracted — public supplier commitment.

Producing — manufacturing underway.

Fielded — delivered to a unit.

Integrated — trained, sustained and connected to plans.

Source shelf

Evidence before brand names.

Official budget documents, parliamentary material, procurement agencies, NATO capability and production documents, company filings and attributed contract releases should anchor each record. Independent research adds context but cannot turn an intention into a delivered capability.
