Product thesis

What is being built?

NATOlinks is an independent, source-led intelligence platform explaining NATO’s founding bargain and tracking whether Europe is building the conventional capability, industrial base and political reliability associated with “NATO 3.0.” It is not an official NATO site, not a generic news blog and not advocacy for one government.

Core insight

Why now?

On 18 June 2026, Pete Hegseth announced an up-to-six-month review of U.S. force posture and basing in Europe. He argued that Europe must take primary responsibility for its conventional defence; he also alleged that some Allies denied requested basing, port or overflight access during operations against Iran. The site treats those claims as claims requiring documented context—not as settled fact.

Historical frame

1.0 → 2.0 → 3.0

NATO 1.0: Cold War hard-power collective defence. NATO 2.0: post-Cold War expansion, expeditionary activity and European conventional-capability decline. NATO 3.0: contested effort to re-balance responsibility toward Europe while preserving the Alliance. The anchoring sources are the North Atlantic Treaty’s Articles 3 and 5 and early NATO history, including Eisenhower’s SACEUR role.

Data product

What should become structured?

Every record follows: commitment → budget → programme → supplier → production → fielded capability → remaining gap. Launch with six countries—Poland, Germany, France, UK, Netherlands and Sweden—and six capability areas: air defence; deep strike; drones/counter-drone; ammunition; ISR/refuelling; mobility/logistics.

Review questions

Please pressure-test these points

  1. Does the NATO 1.0 / 2.0 / 3.0 frame explain the debate fairly, or does it need another period or label?
  2. Which primary sources should anchor every historical and current claim?
  3. How should the site distinguish a government assertion, an independently verified fact and an analyst inference?
  4. Is “Alliance Reliability” useful and measurable from public evidence without implying access to classified arrangements?
  5. Which first buyer has the highest willingness to pay: supplier, adviser, investor, journalist or think tank?
  6. What is the smallest data set that makes the Monitor valuable before full coverage?

Non-negotiable guardrails

Do not use NATO emblems or imply official affiliation. Use NATO media only within its stated editorial-use terms and retain required credit. Cite sources by record. Do not make operationally sensitive claims. Separate fact, claim and inference in the page design.
